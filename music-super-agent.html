Why I built this

The creative bottleneck isn't skill. It's systems.

"I spent 23 years in product leadership at Walmart, Amazon, and Microsoft building systems for millions of users. In 2026, I started pursuing this passion — understanding AI agents from the inside, not as a PM reading docs, but as someone debugging 3am errors in a Selenium session."

Music was the domain. I've always had strong taste and emotional connection to Indian classical, Bollywood, and fusion. I don't play an instrument or produce audio. That's the point.

This project is also a real-world evals framework. CTR is evidence about packaging; average view duration is evidence about post-click consumption; watch time per impression combines the two. Every release is a test whose result informs the next creative decision. The channel is the testbed.

How it works

Idea to published video. One pipeline. One hour.

The pipeline covers the full content lifecycle — from audio generation to cross-platform distribution. The next layer adds a creative intelligence system that decides what to make and how to make it, learning creator taste over time.

Distribution is already extending beyond the initial subscriber base
Browse features and Suggested videos delivered 142 combined views in 6 days starting from 3 subscribers. Encouraging — but batch 1 is still too small to infer a durable growth rate.
Saanson Ki Maala looks like a two-stage problem
552 impressions and 4.53% CTR points to weak packaging. But ~25% estimated average percentage viewed suggests post-click consumption is also weak. Change the thumbnail first, then measure whether CTR improves while watching whether retention changes independently.
One hashtag produced a 9.3% like rate — worth testing further
Pal Pal produced 30 likes (9.3% like rate), far above the rest of the batch. Song choice, clip, posting time and opening frame are all confounds. Repeating the pattern across future releases before encoding it as strategy.
Cross-platform reach is promising; attribution is not yet measured
Two "Full video on YouTube" reels received 413 Instagram views. That is promising reach, but not proof of 413 YouTube referrals. Instrumenting reel → profile click → YouTube external referral → watch time is the next measurement priority.
Walk Through the Storm is the strongest combined signal
The original pairs 15.45% CTR with ~86% estimated average percentage viewed plus clean original IP. One video is not enough to prove originals win — but it is the most important hypothesis to replicate.

Questions

How this works

Plain answers for people who aren't reading the code.

You start with a song idea. An AI music platform generates the audio. The pipeline picks it up from there — downloads it, builds an animated music video, writes the YouTube title, description and tags, and uploads it automatically. The only thing it asks you to do is approve the sync before the final render. Start to published in about 20 minutes.
No. The audio is AI-generated from a text prompt. The creative contribution is taste: which songs to make, what mood, what lyrics, what visual direction. The pipeline handles everything between "I have an idea" and "it's on YouTube."
The current pipeline is mechanical — it automates the same steps in the same order every time. The next layer adds creative judgment: a system that reads a persistent profile of the creator's taste and plans each video individually — what shots to use, what mood to convey, what to avoid — before any assets are generated. The goal is that the system gets better at making your kind of video over time, not just faster at making videos.
Three things right now: approving the video before the final render, generating cover art when the AI music platform's default image isn't right, and posting the Instagram reel. The caption is written automatically, but you still tap Post. Everything else runs unattended.
The algorithm is pushing it to more people than any other video — 552 impressions — but only 4.53% click through, vs 15–16% for the top performers. That gap is almost certainly the thumbnail: it's not stopping the scroll. There's also a secondary issue — people who do click are only watching about 25% of the video on average, so there may be something in the content itself to address separately.
About $145/month in subscriptions across the tools used for audio generation, visual generation, video processing, and publishing. At the current pace of 12 videos a month that works out to roughly $12 per video. Most of the spend is fixed infrastructure, so the per-video cost drops significantly as output scales.
Not yet. It is a working personal pipeline that proves the concept: a solo creator with no audio or video background can publish at studio output velocity using AI agents. The interesting open question is what the right product shape looks like — and that's partly what the feedback section below is exploring.
