Motion Data Pivot Open questions

AI portfolio · product discovery · Sep 2026

MusicSuperAgent → Keepsake Studio

An AI pipeline that takes a song idea to a published music video in under an hour, built to test one question: is a family member's voice, finished beautifully, something people actually want to keep? Four weeks of data said yes to the question and no to the first answer, so I changed what I was building.

Overview deck (PDF, 7 slides) ⤓ Watch the walkthrough ↓

The question

Most AI-music tools compete on generation quality: better prompts, better voices, faster iteration. That is a commodity race, and roughly 75,000 new AI tracks already land on Deezer every day. It was never the interesting question.

The narrower one: who is this for, and what makes it worth keeping? I used my own YouTube channel as the instrument. Twenty-six videos, two 28-day windows of platform data, and a read of how people reacted rather than how they said they would.

What was built

An end-to-end pipeline, not a demo. One human checkpoint: I approve lyric sync before the final render.

  1. Song idea
    brief + voice
  2. Audio & art
    AI music, cover art
  3. Video
    FFmpeg: motion, waveform, lyrics
  4. Human sync check
    approve before render
  5. Publish
    YouTube, Instagram

Two agents, one provider layer

A Director agent plans the creative (taste profile, emotional read, shot plan). A Producer agent executes assets behind a provider abstraction, with a QA and retry loop and approved-asset locking so nothing is silently regenerated.

Cost-routed on purpose

Lyric polishing runs on a small, cheap model (Claude Haiku); it does not need a frontier one. Per-video cost: $0.02–0.69, depending on whether cover art comes free from the audio platform. About $145 a month all-in at 12 videos, dominated by subscriptions rather than usage.

Motion

The finishing layer is the product: a look, a slow zoom, film grain, lyrics timed to the music, a moving waveform and a portrait badge. Both clips are silent and do not autoplay.

Reach is borrowed from the song

A cover of a famous classic drew 46,405 impressions at a 2.8% click-through and 18% average viewed. An original piece with a far better engagement profile (99% average viewed) got a few hundred views. The algorithm routes through songs people already search for; it does not amplify quality it cannot place in an existing pool.

Completion tracks personal connection

Viewers aged 45–54 are 9.7% of views but 17.5% of watch time, watching about 46% of each video against roughly 25% for everyone else. The highest-completion songs are all family or occasion pieces, and all low-reach. Famous songs buy impressions; personal ones hold attention.

Production spend did not move it

A cinematic AI-video cut of one song underperformed its plain sibling. More polish on a distribution problem is wasted effort.

Nothing is passed on

No Instagram comments and almost no shares in 30 days. People watch, but nothing travels person to person yet. That is the measure that matters for a keepsake: forwards, not views.

The pivot: build for the maker, not the audience

Twelve more videos would have sharpened a distribution question, not the one I was asking. So I stopped shipping and changed the product. The pieces that land are made for a specific person or occasion, and they succeed by being finished and shared inside a family, not by being found. The people who make them already have the audio. What is missing is the last step.

Keepsake Studio is that last step: a private, in-browser studio that turns a recording into a finished, shareable video. Free, self-serve, invited testers first. To try it, get in touch and I will send you a link.

Decisions worth naming

  • Self-serve, not hand-delivered. The maker does everything; my contribution is tooling and taste (four looks, a curated scene library rooted in Indian settings and the retro mood people already respond to).
  • One static page, no backend. Rendering runs on the maker's own machine (canvas plus the browser's recorder). Running cost is close to zero, and music, photos and dedications never leave the computer, which matters for unpublished or deeply personal recordings. It also removes accounts and data storage.
  • Accessibility and privacy as defaults. Built to WCAG 2.2 AA; no tracking; a plain-language tip on every control.
  • Trade-off accepted: desktop-first, one piece at a time. Any sharing between people would need a backend; that is deliberately deferred.
  • Platform risk, named. No public API exists for the music generation this depends on. The Suno developer-programme application is in, with no answer yet, so the tool hands off to it by copy and paste today.

What the first test measures

One real maker, working unaided: can he go from file to finished video without help, where does he stop or ask, does he share the result, and would he make a second one? The success measure is forwards inside the family, not views.

Open, honestly

This is mid-discovery, not a finished case study.

  • Whether "the finishing layer for personal music" is a real, narrow starting point or too thin is untested beyond a first invited maker.
  • Repeat use is unknown: what would make someone come back for a second and third piece.
  • Spread is unknown: how a tool like this travels beyond one family without marketing.
  • The channel is a poor test bed for original work, so some of these answers can only come from real makers, not analytics.
